# PotongKu

A booking app for barbers. Barbers sign up, list their services, chairs and hours, and share a booking link. Customers find a barber, pick a service, barber and time, and book without calling or DMing. Launching in Kajang first.

One app serves both sides: people choose "Customer" or "Barber / shop owner" when they sign up.

## What's in phase 1

**Everyone**
- Email and password sign-in, with password reset by emailed code
- Delete account from the Account screen (required by the App Store and Google Play)
- English or Bahasa Melayu: follows the phone's language, with a switch on the welcome screen and under Account
- Easy to read and use with big text or a screen reader. Text size under Account (and on the welcome and shop set-up screens): Normal, Large or Largest (1, 1.25 or 1.5 times), kept on the phone and on top of the phone's own text size on iPhone and Android; on the web, where the phone's setting never reaches the page, the choice is what makes it bigger. At Largest on a 360-wide phone, in English and Malay, every screen fits: rows and buttons wrap instead of running off the side, line heights grow with the words, and prices, times and names show whole. The day as chairs and the Takings charts stop at 1.25 times so blocks and bars keep their shape (a long name in a short booking is cut short there, but reads out whole and opens in full), tab labels at 1.4 so four still fit, and screen titles at 1.15 so a long Malay one fits beside Back. TalkBack and VoiceOver hear what each button, chip, tab and card is and does, and whether it is picked, turned off or busy; icons are silent, titles are headings, and a mistake in a form is read out as it appears. Text and icon colours pass WCAG AA in light and dark (4.5:1 for words, 3:1 for edges, icons and bars), which `npm test` checks
- Opens at once on a slow line: Find a barber, My bookings, the barber's Bookings tab and My shop's Takings, Customers, Reviews and Holidays show what they showed last time straight away (kept on the phone for a day, for that account only, and forgotten on sign out or account deletion), with a small spinner by the title while the server's answer comes. If that fails the old answer stays, with "Couldn't refresh. Showing what you saw at 3:05 pm." and Try again; buttons that change a booking wait for the fresh list. A first visit shows grey shapes of what is coming, and a shop opened from the list shows its name, stars and address while the rest loads. In the demo, with every request taking 1.5 seconds, opening the app again shows the barbers in 0.2 seconds instead of 3.5, and My bookings in 0.1 seconds instead of 1.9

**Customers**
- Browse live barbershops, see which are open now and when each can next take you, and search by name or area

It is that or an area, not both; a search still filters, and what it finds stays nearest first. It is remembered for next time and asks for a fresh position then, while the customer's next cut and barbers show at once. If location is refused or can't be found, one line says so and how to allow it, and the list stays as it was; on a web page that can't have location at all (the demo framed by another site without `allow="geolocation"`), it says that instead and to pick an area. The shop's page shows the distance by its address
- Coming back is one tap: Find a barber starts with their next cut and up to three of their barbers, each with the last cut there, when it is next free and Book again with the same service and barber already picked (or that it isn't taking bookings). The one they went to last says "Due for a cut" once their usual gap there has passed with nothing booked
- Book a service with a chosen barber or "any barber", from free 15-minute slots over the next 14 days, with an optional note for the barber
- Pick a barber by more than a name: each is a card with the line their shop wrote about them ("Skin fades and beard trims, cutting since 2015") and, from three reviews of their own cuts, their stars (4.8 ★ (12)); a barber with no line or few reviews shows just the name. "Any barber" stays first and picked, and the cards come in the request the page already sends
- Pick a time first and sign up after; the chosen slot is kept
- A full day isn't the end: under "No free times this day", "Tell the shop I'd take a cancellation" puts them on that shop's waiting list for the day, with the service and barber (or any) they picked; a guest signs up first and the pick is kept. Nothing is promised: it says the shop may WhatsApp them if a time frees up and decides who gets it. Joined, the page says so with Leave, and My bookings lists each open request above the upcoming bookings ("Waiting for a time on Sat, 24 Oct", "Haircut, any barber", the shop) with See free times and Leave. A request goes once its day has passed, they book that day at that shop, or the shop puts them in by hand with their number. A barber's whole day off shows on the day strip as Off for that barber (or Closed, with any barber, when nobody else who works that weekday is in), so the page never offers to wait for a day that can't free up; if the day goes off while the page is open, the card says "That barber is off that day. Try any barber." instead of the button. The page only asks for their list when the day they picked turns out full
- Walk-in queue: at a shop that takes walk-ins in turn, Find a barber says "3 waiting" or "No queue" by its open-now line, and while it is open its page has a card above the booking steps ("3 waiting, about 40 min") with Join the queue: a service, and a barber in now or any, with no time to pick; a guest signs up first and joins on coming back. Once in, the card and the top of My bookings show their place ("You're 4th in the queue · about 55 min"), asked again every 30 seconds while they wait and read out as it moves, with Leave. When the shop calls them, both say "It's your turn. Head to the shop now." with Directions; once their cut starts it is an ordinary booking. One queue at a time, none at a shop where they have a booking still to come today, and at most 30 waiting
- See upcoming and past bookings, move one to another time, cancel, WhatsApp the shop, book again
- Rate a cut from one to five stars, with an optional comment, once the shop has marked it done: My bookings asks "How was your cut?" for 14 days after it, and the rating can be changed in that time. The form says which name the review will show (the name they go by, worked out from their full name: "Mohd Hakim bin Ali" is Hakim, "Tan Wei Jie" is Wei Jie, never a surname) and lets them change it. The shop's reply shows under it
- A shop with three or more reviews shows its average and count (4.8 ★ (23)) in the list and by its name; its page lists the reviews below the booking steps, newest first, each with the customer's chosen name, the barber, the comment and the shop's reply. When two or more of its barbers have reviews, chips above them ("Everyone · Ali · Danial") narrow them to one barber's, with that barber's stars, still newest first and a page at a time
- Add a booking to the phone's calendar, so it reminds them: Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac gets a calendar file for Apple Calendar (with an alert an hour before); Android, the iOS app and other browsers open Google Calendar's add-event page (with their usual Google reminder)

- Barbers (one per chair) with weekly working hours and an optional daily break (e.g. Friday prayers); a solo barber is a shop with one chair. - Bookings by day with expected takings, refreshed every minute and by pulling down; mark done or no-show once the time has started, move a booking to another free time when the customer calls (it stays theirs, and a WhatsApp tells them the new time), cancel, WhatsApp the customer

Tapping a booking gives the same actions as in the list; tapping free time opens Add to the day with that barber and quarter hour picked. Two chairs fit on a small phone and more scroll sideways; a wide window fits them all
- What customers changed since you last looked: the Bookings tab shows how many bookings customers made, moved or cancelled (checked every minute and when the app comes back), and a card at the top lists them, e.g. "Wei Jie cancelled today at 5:30 pm with Ali, so that chair is free". Each line opens its day; new bookings say New until Got it, which each phone remembers by the newest change it was sent
- Waiting for a time: on a day with customers on its waiting list, the Bookings tab has a card "Waiting for a time (2)", oldest first, each with what they asked for ("Skin fade with Danial", "Haircut, any barber") and when they asked, above the list or the chairs (and only those for the barber shown, or anyone, on a phone set to one barber). Once a time frees up that fits one, the row says so ("11:30 am with Ali is free"), and its WhatsApp offers it: "Hi Hakim, an 11:30 am Haircut with Ali just opened on Sat, 24 Oct at Ali Barber Sungai Chua. Call in marks them called, so their phone says it is their turn, and with a number opens WhatsApp with "Hi Hakim, it's your turn at Ali Barber Sungai Chua. See you in a few minutes!". Start picks the barber (the one they asked for if in, else the first one free, each chip saying Free, Busy until 3:45 pm or Booked 4:00 pm) and a service if none was given, and makes the cut a booking from now; a barber busy before it would end is named so another can be picked. Not here takes them off, and + Add walk-in adds someone by name, with their number and what they want if they say. The queue comes with the rest of the day, refreshed every minute, and only those for the barber shown (or any) on a phone set to one barber
- WhatsApp reminders the day before: one tap opens WhatsApp with the message written, and every phone in the shop sees who has been reminded (moving a booking clears it; one that never went out can be taken back). Today's view says how many of tomorrow's customers are still to remind. Customers who booked today, or are coming later today, can still be reminded from More
- Customer list from My shop: everyone who has booked or been in, online or added by the shop (walk-ins are matched by phone number however it was typed, including to an online customer with that number), with their number, visits, last cut and next booking. Customers due for a cut (their usual gap between cuts has passed and nothing is booked) come first, with a ready-written WhatsApp invite that includes the booking link, which is also there for anyone away much longer; anyone with a number can be messaged or called. Searchable by name or number
- Add walk-in, WhatsApp or phone bookings, and block time or a whole day off (or close the whole shop for the day), so online customers can't take those times
- Takings for this week, last week or this month: money in and cuts done against the same point in the period before (once the shop has been on the app that long), no-shows and cancellations, the busiest days (by the average day) and hours, each barber's share and the top services. My shop shows the week so far in one line
- Reviews from My shop: the average, how many and the newest comment, then every review with the cut it was for. The owner can reply to each (shown under it on the shop's page), and change or remove the reply. - Go live / pause, share or copy the booking link; while paused, the link asks customers to WhatsApp the shop, and My shop says the shop is paused (bookings already made still stand) rather than new

- One-month free trial on every new shop. From five days before it ends, My shop (and the poster once it has ended) has a button that WhatsApps the PotongKu team, with the shop's name and link written, to subscribe

**Rules enforced in the database**
- A barber can never be double-booked (Postgres exclusion constraint)
- Bookings are only created through `book_appointment` (customers) and `add_shop_booking` (owners), and moved through `reschedule_booking` (the customer, or the shop's owner), which re-check the slot is free and inside working hours
- Customers only see their own bookings; owners only see their own shop's bookings and customers
- Takings come from one owner-only `shop_summary` call over at most 93 days, counted at the price each booking was made at
- My shop's cards come from one owner-only `shop_cards` call: the period's takings as `shop_summary` totals them, how many customers `shop_customers` lists and how many are due (counted by `shop_customer_counts` without making the list; nobody can call it on its own), the review count, total and newest comment, and the days closed from today to 60 days ahead. The database tests check each number against the call it replaces
- What customers changed comes from one owner-only `shop_changes` call: only what customers did themselves (the booking functions note it; the shop's own changes never count), for bookings from today on, at most a week back
- Shops are only visible while published and paid up or inside the free trial
- A shop only goes live, or back on after a pause, with at least one active service and an active barber with working hours; otherwise the database refuses and says which is missing. A new shop always starts hidden. What a shop still needs comes from one owner-only `shop_setup` call (how many active services, and each active barber with whether they have hours), which the app sends only while the shop isn't live or its steps still show
- A shop's location is both latitude and longitude or neither, and on the map; only its owner sets it. Distances are worked out inside `find_shops` (great-circle, with `km_between`, which nobody can call on its own). The customer's position is never stored or logged: the app rounds it to about 100 m, keeps it in memory only, and sends it in the request's body, never in the URL that access logs keep. Don't turn on logging of every statement with its values (`log_statement = 'all'`, or `log_min_duration_statement` low enough to catch `find_shops`), as that would log it
- Owners can't change their own trial or subscription status, or when their shop first went live (`shops.published_at`, set by the database)
- A barber account can turn into a customer one (`become_customer`) only while it has no shop
- Limits against abuse: bookings at most 60 days ahead, at most 4 upcoming bookings per customer per shop, notes, review comments and replies up to 280 characters
- Only the customer can rate a cut, one rating per booking, once it is marked done and up to 14 days after it started (`rate_booking`); owners can't rate their own shop. Nobody can read which account wrote a review, only the name it shows. Owners only reply (`reply_to_review`), and the database keeps each shop's review count and total
- The database also keeps each barber's review count and total (`barbers.rating_count`, `rating_sum`), from reviews of cuts with that barber: written, changed or deleted (with the customer's account, too), and when a booking's barber changes, its review and stars move with it. Nobody can write them, owners included; per barber there are only counts and totals, never who rated. A barber's intro is one line of plain text, up to 60 characters, set by the shop's owner
- Waiting lists: customers join through `join_waitlist` only while the shop is live and the day is bookable (not closed, not gone by, at most 60 days ahead) for an active service, one request per customer per shop per day, none on a day they already have a booking there, and at most 3 requests at a time. Nobody can read or write the `waitlist` table: customers see and leave their own (`my_waitlist`, which `my_bookings` also sends, and `leave_waitlist`), and the owner sees a day's list with each customer's name and number as the customer list shows them (`shop_waitlist`), marks one offered (`mark_waitlist_offered`) or takes it off (`leave_waitlist`). Booking or moving to that day at that shop ends the customer's request there, and so does the owner adding a booking that day (`add_shop_booking`) with the number on the customer's profile, compared in the one form `shop_customers` uses. `join_waitlist` also refuses a day the barber asked for has off ("That barber is off that day. Try any barber."); `shop_closed_days` lists each barber's own whole days off (with `barber_id`, and the reason only for the owner) besides the days nobody who works that weekday is in, so the booking page shows them before anyone asks. Days gone by drop out of every list and stop counting towards the limit, without a scheduled job
- Walk-in queues: nobody can read or write `queue_entries`. Customers join today's queue through `join_queue` only while the shop is live, its queue is on and a barber is in now (working, not on a break or a whole day off), with the barber they ask for in; one place at a time across every shop, none where they have a booking still to come that day, and at most 30 waiting. They see a shop's count, wait and their own place (`queue_status`, also for guests, who see counts but never who), their own place anywhere (`my_queue`, also inside `my_bookings`), and leave (`leave_queue`). The owner lists today's queue with names and numbers as the customer list shows them (`shop_queue`), adds walk-ins (`add_to_queue`, only with the queue on), calls (`call_from_queue`), takes off (`remove_from_queue`) and starts a cut (`start_from_queue`): a booking from now for the service's length with a barber in now and free until it ends, made in the same lock as every other booking. Find a barber's count comes from `find_shops`. Days gone by drop out of every list without a scheduled job
- Barbers with booking history can't be deleted (mark them away instead), so past bookings keep their barber; one added by mistake can be removed

## Tech

- [Expo](https://expo.dev) SDK 57 with Expo Router (iOS, Android and web from one codebase)
- [Supabase](https://supabase.com) for sign-in, Postgres and row-level security
- Schema and functions: [`supabase/migrations`](supabase/migrations)

## Trying it without Supabase

```bash
npm run build:demo
```

This builds `dist-demo/index.html`: the whole web app in one file, with a pretend backend inside the page ([`src/demo`](src/demo)) and four sample shops around Kajang. Open it in any browser, or host it anywhere, and tap **Try as a customer** (Hakim) or **Try as a barber** (Ali of Ali Barber Sungai Chua). Nothing is sent anywhere; changes are kept in that browser until **Start the demo again**. The demo password reset code is `123456`.

To see it on a weak signal, run `localStorage.setItem('potongku.demo.network', '{"delayMs":1500}')` (or `'{"offline":true}'`) in the browser console and reload; remove the key to put it back.

The pretend backend copies the database rules in `supabase/migrations` (row-level security, the booking functions and their messages), so when you change a rule there, change it in `src/demo` too. `npm test` checks the demo backend through the real Supabase client.

## Getting it running

1. Create a free project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com).
2. Apply the database schema, either:
   - paste `supabase/migrations/20261005000000_init.sql` into the Supabase SQL editor and run it, or
   - with the Supabase CLI: `supabase link --project-ref <ref>` then `supabase db push`.
3. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill in the project URL and anon key (Supabase: Project Settings > API).
4. Install and start:

   ```bash
   npm install
   npx expo start
   ```

   Scan the QR code with the Expo Go app on your phone, or press `w` for the web version.

Tip for testing: in Supabase under Authentication > Sign In / Providers > Email, you can turn off "Confirm email" so new accounts can sign in straight away.

### Emails (password reset)

"Forgot password?" emails a 6-digit code instead of a link, so it works the same in the app and on the web. Two settings in Supabase under Authentication > Emails:

1. In the **Reset password** template, add the code, for example: `<p>Your PotongKu code is <strong>{{ .Token }}</strong></p>`.
2. Before launch, set up **SMTP settings** with an email provider (Resend, Brevo, etc.). Supabase's built-in sender only delivers to your own team's addresses and a few emails an hour.

## Checks

```bash
npm run typecheck   # TypeScript
npm run lint        # ESLint
npm test            # date, money, phone, reminder, distance, language and text size helpers, colour contrast, and the demo backend
npm run test:db     # schema + booking rules against a throwaway local Postgres 16+
npm run test:e2e    # the whole app in a browser: barber sets up, customers book
npm run test:demo   # the one-file demo, in a locked-down iframe with no network
npm run test:load   # Malaysia-sized data, the busiest screens under load and a booking rush
```

`test:db` needs `initdb`, `pg_ctl` and `psql` installed locally (it does not need Supabase or Docker).

`test:e2e` builds the web app and runs it against a local stand-in for Supabase (Postgres, [PostgREST](https://github.com/PostgREST/postgrest/releases) 12 and a small auth gateway in `e2e/backend`). It needs Postgres 16+, the PostgREST binary on your `PATH` (or `POSTGREST=/path/to/postgrest`) and Playwright's Chromium (`npx playwright install chromium`). Set `SCREENSHOTS=<folder>` to save a screenshot of each step there, and `COLOR_SCHEME=dark` to run in dark mode.

GitHub Actions runs all of these on every push (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) except `test:load`, which takes a few minutes and needs the same Postgres and PostgREST as `test:e2e`.

## How many users it handles

`npm run test:load` fills a local Postgres with 1,000 live shops (three chairs each), 100,000 customers, about 2.9 million bookings (90 days of history and the next 30 days) and 280,000 reviews, sends each screen's own requests 50 at a time for 15 seconds, then has 300 customers try to book the same shop's Saturday evening at once. On a 4-core machine that also runs the load generator:

| Screen | Requests a second | Typical (p50) | Slow (p95) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Find a barber, signed in (open now, next free time, your next cut and your barbers) | 132 | 335 ms | 854 ms |
| Barbers near me (the same list, nearest first) | 142 | 309 ms | 771 ms |
| Search for a barber | 269 | 136 ms | 504 ms |
| Shop page (with closed days, today's hours, each barber's stars and the newest reviews) | 230 | 211 ms | 293 ms |
| Free times for a day | 965 | 49 ms | 75 ms |
| Book a cut (free times, then book) | 541 | 94 ms | 130 ms |
| My bookings (with ratings and waiting lists) | 435 | 105 ms | 202 ms |
| Barber's day (with what customers changed and who is waiting) | 203 | 240 ms | 345 ms |
| My shop (takings, customers, reviews, closed days and the set-up steps) | 299 | 162 ms | 240 ms |
| Join and leave a waiting list | 662 | 76 ms | 110 ms |

In the rush, six customers got the last evening times and the other 294 were told the time was just taken, all within half a second and with no errors.

Find a barber is the slowest customer screen because it works out each listed shop's hours today and next free time, and for a signed-in customer also their own barbers'. Even so, one such machine serves it nearly half a million times an hour. Runs on the same machine vary by about 15%: these numbers are from one run, so compare screens with each other rather than with an earlier round's table.

In round 13 the busiest screens' requests got cheaper without changing what they return: a script sent about 2,800 varied requests to the old and new functions at each of twelve times of day, including a few minutes either side of midnight, and every answer matched. Three things took most of the shop list's time. `find_shops` was plain SQL with settings, which Postgres plans afresh on every call (about 0.45 ms); it then asked for today's hours and the next free time one shop at a time, 20 calls each, and the next free time alone took nearly half the list's time; and a barber's whole day off was found by reading all of that day's bookings. Now `find_shops` picks the page first, straight off a name index (`shops_name_idx`) when nothing narrows the list, and asks for the whole page's hours (`shops_hours_today`) and next free times (`shops_next_free`) in one call each, working out tomorrow only for the shops with nothing left today. Blocks have a small index of their own (`bookings_shop_blocks_idx`), and the functions these screens call are plpgsql with a generic plan, so each connection plans their queries once. On a throwaway Postgres with the load-test data at 3 pm on a Wednesday (median of 300 calls over four connections, sent the way PostgREST sends them), database time per request:

- Find a barber's list: 3.3 ms instead of 10.2 signed in (p95 4.8 instead of 13.4), 4.1 instead of 8.9 as a guest; at ten to midnight, when every shop's next free time is tomorrow, 5.0 instead of 11.0.
- Barbers near me 4.2 ms instead of 11.1 (p95 6.6 instead of 15.0), Search 3.7 instead of 8.5, the customer's own barbers (`my_shops`) 1.7 instead of 3.5, and the areas (`shop_areas`) 0.7 instead of 1.4.
- My bookings is one request: `my_bookings` now returns `{bookings, waitlist}`, the waiting list as `my_waitlist` gives it, in 3.0 ms, where the two requests took 5.4 and 0.7.
- The barber's day's waiting list (`shop_waitlist`) 0.23 ms instead of 1.0, and what customers changed (`shop_changes`) 0.29 instead of 0.88. The shop page's closed days (`shop_closed_days`) 0.24 instead of 0.55. Their other requests are plain table reads, and take what they took before.

Against PostgREST on the same data, 50 at a time for 10 seconds, two runs of each: Find a barber went from 154 to 174 visits a second to 254 to 259, Barbers near me from 153 to 181 to 253 to 256, Search from 324 to 349 to 562 to 575, and My bookings from 330 to 334 to 653 to 671. The shop page and the barber's day changed by less than runs vary.

My shop adds up the week's takings and counts the shop's customers over two years, and is only opened by owners. In the full load test it went from 70 visits a second (p50 670 ms) to 337 (p50 142 ms) in this round. Its cards come in one request (`shop_cards`), which counts the customers and those due without making the customer list. On a throwaway Postgres with the load-test data, the cards take 4 ms of database time for a typical shop (2,820 bookings, median of 200 calls), where the four requests they replace took 23 ms, 20 of them making the whole list to read two numbers from it. For a shop with 33,000 bookings in the last two years, a fifth of them walk-ins, they take 78 ms instead of 105 (median of 50); the count reads those two years of bookings, so it grows with a shop's history up to two years and no further. Against PostgREST on the same data, 50 owners at a time for 10 seconds, the "My shop" scenario went from 73 visits a second (p50 680 ms) to 250 (p50 190 ms); one owner at a time, a visit waits 9 ms instead of 28, or 80 ms instead of 116 at the busy shop.

Near me measures the distance to every live shop with a location and keeps the nearest page, with no index. On the 1,000 load-test shops that adds about 1 ms of database time to the list (median of 300 calls from random points around Kajang: 4.2 ms nearest first against 3.3 ms by name; picking the nearest 21 alone takes about 1.1 ms). In `npm run test:load` the "Barbers near me" screen keeps up with Find a barber by name. That is fine for thousands of shops. Past tens of thousands, give the shops a spatial index and search by box or nearest-neighbour instead: Postgres's `earthdistance` extension (a GiST index on `ll_to_earth(latitude, longitude)` and `earth_box` around the customer), or PostGIS (a `geography` column with a GiST index and `ORDER BY location <-> point`). Either needs only `find_shops` changed, not the app.

One barber's reviews are asked for by the barber alone (`reviews?barber_id=eq.…`), which reads them newest first straight off one index (`reviews_barber_created_idx`). With the shop's id as well, Postgres would combine two indexes and sort all of that barber's reviews first. On the load-test data (280,000 reviews; the busiest shop, shop 1, has 3,730), medians over 200 calls as a guest: a random shop's barber, first page 0.090 ms (0.14 ms at p95); shop 1's barbers, first page 0.054 ms and 200 reviews deep 0.76 ms; against 1.8 ms (2.5 ms at p95) for shop 1 with both ids. The owner's Reviews narrowed the same way, with the cut each review is for, takes 0.24 ms at shop 1 and 0.43 ms at a random shop (20 a page). Everyone's reviews are unchanged (0.074 ms), and the shop page's barbers request, now with each barber's intro and stars, takes 0.22 ms; the owner's totals with each barber's take 0.08 ms. The shop page sends nothing new, so its load-test scenario is as before.

A new shop's steps (`shop_setup`) never read bookings: on a throwaway Postgres with 1,001 shops and one shop holding 29,070 bookings, it takes 0.2 ms of database time for that shop (median 0.27 ms over 200 calls, 0.65 ms at most), through the shops, services, barbers and working-hours indexes only. The go-live check adds 0.3 ms to turning bookings on. The load test's "My shop" screen sends it on every visit, though the app only does while a shop is getting ready.

The waiting list is read through two indexes: the owner's day by `waitlist_shop_day_idx` (shop, day, when asked) and a customer's own by the unique (customer, day, shop) key, so lists from days gone by, which nothing deletes, are never read. On a throwaway Postgres with the load-test data (40,340 requests; shop 1 has 1,379, three years of Saturdays) the owner's list for next Saturday takes 0.59 ms of database time at shop 1 (median of 200 calls; 1.17 ms at p95) and 0.47 ms across 200 shops, 0.31 ms for a day nobody waits on and 0.35 ms for a Saturday gone by; `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` shows one index scan of `waitlist_shop_day_idx` returning the day's 3 rows. A customer's own list takes 0.26 ms (My bookings' bookings take 2.5 ms alongside it), joining and leaving 0.51 ms together, and `shop_changes` with its waiting counts 0.25 ms. The shop page sends nothing new on an ordinary visit. Its closed days (`shop_closed_days`) now also say which barber has a day off, and are plpgsql so that each connection plans the query once: as plain SQL it was planned on every call, which took ten times as long as running it. Across 200 load-test shops a call now takes 0.073 ms of database time (median of 400 calls as a guest; 0.13 ms at p95) instead of 0.78 ms, and at shop 1 with five whole days off over three days of its 14, 0.16 ms instead of 0.87; the bookings are read by one range scan of `bookings_shop_starts_idx` over the 14 days (195 rows). The owner adding a booking by hand looks for someone waiting with that number among the day's few requests (`waitlist_shop_day_idx`, then each one's profile by id): 0.11 ms across 200 shops. The load test's "My bookings" scenario now asks for the waiting list too, "Barber's day" its list, and "Join and leave a waiting list" is new. Each of those two screens now sends one more request a visit, which is why the table shows fewer visits a second for them than in round 11 (635 and 263); the database time each adds is under a millisecond, so most of the difference is the extra round trip through PostgREST. Since round 13 My bookings gets its waiting list inside `my_bookings`, so it is one request again (see above); the table is from before that.

The walk-in queue is read through two indexes: a shop's queue today by `queue_entries_shop_day_idx` (shop, day, when joined) and a customer's own by `queue_entries_customer_day_idx` (customer, day), so days gone by, which nothing deletes, are never read. On a throwaway Postgres with the load-test data (one shop in three runs a queue: 542,088 entries, two months of them at every queue shop; shop 3 has 32,580 bookings and 32,136 queue entries, three years of them), medians over 400 calls: the shop page's count and wait (`queue_status`), which a waiting customer's page asks for every 30 seconds, takes 0.10 ms of database time at shop 3 and 0.18 ms across 333 queue shops (0.25 ms at p95), each part one index lookup bounded by the shop or the customer; the customer's place (`my_queue`, which `my_bookings` also sends, so My bookings stays one request) 0.13 ms in a queue and 0.02 ms otherwise; the barber's day (`shop_queue`) 0.05 ms at shop 3. Those two lists are plpgsql, so each connection plans them once: as plain SQL, planning them took ten times as long as running them (0.66 ms against 0.07 ms). Joining and leaving take 0.61 ms together, and adding a walk-in and starting their cut 1.0 ms; starting looks for a booking in the way through the bookings' own overlap index (`bookings_no_overlap`, 0.16 ms). Find a barber's queue counts are one lookup per listed shop that runs a queue: measured against `find_shops` as it was before this round's speed-up, with every list call alternating between the two, the first page went from 9.9 to 10.4 ms (median of 300), Near me from 9.9 to 10.3 ms and a search for Kajang from 8.7 to 9.0 ms. The load test's "Wait in a queue" (join, ask for the place three times, My bookings once, leave) is new, "Shop page" asks for the queue at queue shops and "Barber's day" the queue every time.

That is far more than a pre-Raya peak needs: if all 100,000 customers booked within the same hour, it would be under 30 bookings a second. What it changes is the bill, not the code:

- The data above takes about 1.5 GB, and grows by roughly 4.5 GB a year at that size. Supabase's free plan stops at 500 MB, so a Malaysia-wide launch needs a paid plan; a Kajang launch with a few dozen shops stays under it for its first few years.
- Supabase's built-in email only sends a few messages an hour, so password reset needs your own email provider (custom SMTP) before launch, whatever the size.
- More than 50,000 monthly active sign-ins also needs a paid plan.

## Running the business side by hand (until payments are built)

- A shop's free trial ends 30 days after it is created (`shops.trial_ends_at`).
- When a barber pays, set `subscription_status = 'active'` on their shop in the Supabase table editor.
- When a trial ends without payment, the shop disappears from search and its link stops taking bookings (it tells customers to WhatsApp the shop instead).
- Barbers ask to subscribe from the app's "Keep my shop live" button, which WhatsApps the number in `EXPO_PUBLIC_SUPPORT_WHATSAPP` (set it in `.env` before launch; without it, WhatsApp opens with the message written and asks who to send it to). An EAS build with the `production` profile stops early if this number or the Supabase settings are missing (`scripts/check-release.mjs`).

## Translations

Screens wrap their text in `t('English text')`; the Malay for each line is in [`src/lib/strings-ms.ts`](src/lib/strings-ms.ts), keyed by the English. `npm test` fails if any text is missing a Malay line, so add one whenever you add or change a sentence.

## Renaming the app

The name shown in the app comes from `name` in `app.json`. Also update `slug`, `scheme`, `ios.bundleIdentifier` and `android.package` there before the first store release. The icon, splash and favicon are drawn by `node scripts/make-icons.mjs` (a scissors on the brand red); change `BRAND` there and re-run it.
